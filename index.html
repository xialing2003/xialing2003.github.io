Research

Slow slip modeling

I use a simple discrete elastic model to reproduce the migration patterns and statistical properties of slow slip events. Fault elements are either slipping or locked, with locked elements having either a fully healed static strength or a reduced static strength if they slipped recently. The slip rate is set to be a constant. The model can generate characteristic events with recurrence intervals of about 1 year and propagation speeds of about 10 km/day, consistent with observations and with analytical estimates derived from fracture mechanics. This model also reproduces stochastic properties of Brownian models, with a simplified stochastic linear birth-death model developed to explain the observed proportionality between radiated energy rate and moment rate.

RSQSim simulations

Different from the binary slipping/locked models, all the fault elements evolve following the rate and state friction law. The new simulation considers logarithmic-with-time fault re-strengthening when “locked”, and exponential self-acceleration to instability during nucleation. The RSQSim simulations have the potential to better reproduce the backward propagation patterns and the intermittency observed in seismic records.
